Customers don't only call between nine and five. They call after their own workday, on Sunday afternoon, or the moment something breaks. If your phone goes to a generic voicemail at 9 pm, that caller has to guess whether anyone will ever call back. Many won't wait to find out.

This post is a simple plan for calls that come in when you're closed. Most of it is free and takes less than an hour to set up.

## Why after-hours calls matter

Two published numbers explain the stakes.

- **Calls already go unanswered during the day.** In a 2024 test of 85 small businesses across 58 industries by [411 Locals](https://www.411locals.com/), only 37.8% of calls were answered by a person, and 24.3% got no answer at all. After hours, the share of unanswered calls is close to all of them unless you plan for it.
- **Speed decides a lot.** In a Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million sales leads, ["The Short Life of Online Sales Leads"](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), companies that replied within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as companies that waited longer.

An evening call is often a lead with a clock running. The goal of the plan below is simple: the caller knows what happens next, and you reply fast once you're back.

## Step 1: Make your hours easy to find

Many after-hours calls start with a search. If your hours are wrong on Google, people call when you're closed, or skip you when you're open.

- **Check your regular hours** on your Business Profile. Google explains how in [Edit your business hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300403?hl=en).
- **Add special hours for holidays and one-off closures.** Google's page [How to set special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) shows how to enter them for specific dates, and notes that special hours only appear when your regular hours are set.
- **Match your website and voicemail** to the same hours. Three different answers in three places is worse than none.

## Step 2: Write an after-hours voicemail greeting

Most phones and phone services let you record a separate greeting for after hours. A good one is short and answers the caller's real questions: Did I reach the right place? When will someone call me back? What should I do if it can't wait?

A template you can adapt:

> You've reached [Business name]. We're closed right now. Our hours are [days and times]. Leave your name, number and a short message, and we'll call you back by [time] on our next business day. If this is an emergency, please hang up and call 911.

Say a specific callback time, not "as soon as possible". A promise the caller can hold you to builds more trust than a vague one. Keep the emergency line if there is any chance a caller could need it.

## Step 3: Decide who, if anyone, answers after hours

There is no single right answer. It depends on your work and your budget.

- **Nobody answers, but the greeting is clear.** Fine for many offices and shops, as long as the callback promise is kept.
- **An on-call person.** Common for trades with urgent jobs. Forward calls to that person's phone during set hours, and rotate the duty so nobody burns out.
- **An answering service.** A person takes a message for you. Useful when calls are frequent but you don't need them booked on the spot.
- **An AI receptionist.** It answers every call, takes messages, answers common questions and can book appointments. It should say clearly that it is an AI, and it is not a substitute for emergency services.

Whichever you choose, write it down so everyone on the team knows where after-hours calls go.

## Step 4: Call back first thing

The after-hours plan only works if the morning plan works.

1. Before anything else, open the missed calls and messages from the night.
2. Call back in the order they came in, or urgent ones first.
3. If you can't reach someone, send a short text or leave a voicemail with a time you'll try again.
4. Keep the callback promise from your greeting. If you said "by 10 am", call by 10 am.

The Harvard Business Review numbers above are about reply time. The faster you call back, the better your odds.

## Step 5: Check once a week

Five minutes on Monday is enough:

- How many calls came in after hours last week?
- How many got a callback by the time your greeting promised?
- Did anyone call during a holiday or closure that your Google hours didn't show?
- Is your greeting still correct?

If after-hours calls keep growing, that is a sign to look at an answering service or an AI receptionist rather than longer days.

## A simple after-hours checklist

- Regular and special hours correct on Google
- Same hours on your website
- After-hours greeting with your hours, a callback time and the emergency line
- A written rule for who handles after-hours calls
- Morning callback routine, in order
- A weekly five-minute check
