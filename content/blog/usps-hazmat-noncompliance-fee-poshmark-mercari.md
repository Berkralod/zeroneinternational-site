Since July 12, 2026, the US Postal Service has charged a **$50 HAZMAT noncompliance fee** on commercial shipments when it finds hazardous-materials markings or contents that were not properly declared on the label. For most resellers the risk is not that they ship dangerous goods. It is that they reuse a box that still carries an old hazmat marking.

Until now, Poshmark absorbed most of that charge for its sellers. That ends on **October 19, 2026**. From that date, if USPS flags a Poshmark shipment, the full $50 comes out of the seller's earnings. Here is what changed, how the main marketplaces handle the fee, and a short checklist to keep it from happening to you.

## What the fee is

The fee comes from a USPS rate filing published in the Federal Register. It applies to competitive commercial products (the services most marketplace labels use, including Ground Advantage and Priority Mail) when hazardous materials are detected but the shipper did not declare and label them correctly.

USPS checks packages with its automated package verification systems. A flag can be triggered by:

- hazmat markings on the outside of the box that do not match the label data, and
- hazmat contents (for example certain aerosols, perfumes, nail polish or lithium batteries) shipped without the right declaration.

The first case is the one that catches most resellers by surprise. A box that once held a shipment of perfume or batteries may still carry a small black-and-white "Limited Quantity" diamond or a UN marking. If you reuse that box for a sweater, the package can be flagged even though nothing inside is hazardous.

## How the marketplaces handle it

Each marketplace that sells you a prepaid label decides who pays when USPS charges the fee back.

**Poshmark.** Poshmark covered the full fee when it started on July 12. From August 1 it charged sellers $5 and absorbed the other $45. Its help center and an October 5 blog post confirm that this arrangement runs through October 18. **From October 19, 2026, the full $50 is deducted from seller earnings** when USPS flags a shipment as noncompliant.

**Mercari.** Mercari splits the fee. Since September 1, sellers pay $25 of the $50 and Mercari covers the rest when an improperly marked package is flagged.

**Other platforms and your own labels.** If you buy labels directly or through shipping software, assume the fee lands on you. Check your label provider's billing terms for "adjustments" or "noncompliance", because that is where these charges usually appear.

## Why this matters for small sellers

On a typical used-clothing sale, $50 can be more than the item's entire profit. One flagged package can wipe out the margin on several sales. Because the most common trigger is packaging rather than contents, the fix is mostly a habit change in how you prepare boxes.

## Checklist: avoid the $50 fee

Use this before every shipment, especially if you reuse packaging.

1. **Inspect every reused box on all six sides.** Look for the black-and-white "Limited Quantity" diamond, UN numbers, "ORM-D", lithium battery marks, flammable symbols or old shipping labels with hazmat codes.
2. **Remove or fully cover old markings.** Peel them off, or cover them completely with opaque tape or a plain label. A partly visible mark can still be read by the scanner.
3. **When in doubt, use a new box or poly mailer.** A new mailer costs far less than the fee. Keep a small stock of plain packaging for exactly this reason.
4. **Know which of your items are hazardous.** Perfume, nail polish, aerosols, some cosmetics and items with lithium batteries can be regulated. Check the marketplace's hazardous-materials policy before you list them, and follow the USPS rules if you ship them at all.
5. **Don't ship regulated items on a standard label.** If an item must be declared, use a shipping method that lets you declare it, or don't sell it on a platform whose labels cannot.
6. **Check your earnings statements weekly.** Look for unexpected deductions or label adjustments. If you see a hazmat charge, check which order it came from and what box you used, so the same mistake isn't repeated.
7. **Tell anyone who packs for you.** If a family member, assistant or prep service ships orders, make sure they know the rule. One reused box is enough to trigger the fee.

## What to do if you get charged

Look for the deduction in your marketplace earnings or label provider's billing history and note the order number. Compare it with the item and packaging you used. If you believe the package was compliant, contact the marketplace's support team with photos of the packaging if you have them, and ask what review process is available. Either way, record the cause so you can fix the habit behind it.

## The bigger lesson: track fees per order

Shipping adjustments, conversion charges and policy fees often arrive days after a sale, buried in a statement. If you only look at gross sales, they are easy to miss. A simple sheet that records fees per order, by platform, shows you quickly when a new charge starts eating into your margin.
