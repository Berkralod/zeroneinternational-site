eBay is raising the **seller currency conversion charge** by a quarter of a percentage point. According to eBay's own selling fees page, the charge for US-registered sellers rises from 3% to **3.25% on October 14, 2026**. Other markets follow over the next weeks. Several seller-news sites noted that the change appeared on eBay's fee pages without a separate Seller Update announcement, so many sellers may not have seen it.

A quarter point sounds small. Whether it matters to you depends entirely on how much of your business involves a currency other than your own. Here is what changes, who pays it and what to check this week.

## What is changing, and when

Based on eBay's fee pages and reporting by seller-news sites:

- **United States:** 3% → 3.25% from October 14, 2026 (reports say Canada changes on the same date).
- **Australia:** increase from November 1, 2026.
- **United Kingdom:** 2.5% → 2.75% from December 1, 2026.
- **Germany, France, Italy and Spain:** 3% → 3.25% from December 2, 2026.

Always confirm the rate for your own country on eBay's selling fees page, because the help pages are updated per market.

## When the charge applies

This is *not* a fee on every sale. The currency conversion charge only comes into play when eBay has to convert money between currencies, for example:

- **You list or sell on another eBay site** that uses a different currency, such as a US seller listing on eBay Germany. Fees may be collected in that currency and your proceeds converted back to your payout currency.
- **A buyer pays in a currency other than your payout currency**, where eBay offers that option. The amount is converted before it is paid out to you.

eBay's transaction exchange rate is made up of a base exchange rate plus this conversion charge, which eBay keeps. The rate is captured at the time of sale and shown in the order details, and the same rate is used if you later refund the order.

It is also separate from other cross-border costs. eBay's fee pages list an **international fee** for sales to buyers in other countries, which has its own rules and exemptions. Do not mix the two up when you check your margins.

## Who should pay attention

- **Sellers who actively list on eBay sites in other countries.** If you use multi-site listings to reach buyers in Europe, the UK or Australia, every one of those sales is affected.
- **High-value international sellers.** The increase is 0.25% of the converted amount. As an illustration, on $50,000 of converted sales a year that is an extra $125 compared with the old rate, on top of what you already paid.
- **Sellers with thin margins** on cross-border items, such as trading cards, collectibles or electronics, where a few dollars per order decide whether a sale is worth it.

If you only list on your home eBay site and sell in your own currency, you will most likely never see this charge.

## What to check this week

1. **Find out how much of your revenue is converted.** In your eBay reports or order details, look at orders from other eBay sites or paid in other currencies. Add up the last 3–6 months to estimate your yearly converted volume.
2. **Recalculate your margins for international listings.** Update any spreadsheet or pricing rule that assumes the old rate. If you use a profit calculator, check whether it already includes the new percentage.
3. **Review your international pricing.** For items listed on other eBay sites, consider whether a small price adjustment or a minimum price threshold is needed so cross-border sales stay profitable after all fees.
4. **Compare your options for reaching international buyers.** Depending on your category, offering international shipping from your home site may lead to different fees than listing directly on a foreign eBay site. Check eBay's current fee pages for your situation before changing your setup.
5. **Note the effective dates.** If you sell in several markets, put the dates above in your calendar so your reports make sense when the rate changes mid-quarter.

Fee changes like this rarely arrive with fanfare. Marketplace fee pages change, and the only reliable way to notice is to measure what you actually pay.
