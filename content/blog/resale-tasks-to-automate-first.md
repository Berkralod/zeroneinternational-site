Most resale businesses hit the same wall: sales grow, and so does the pile of small, repetitive tasks around every item. Hiring helps, but a lot of that work is better done by software. The question is where to start.

## How to choose what to automate

Before looking at specific tasks, score your candidates on three questions:

1. **How often does it happen?** A task you repeat for every item or order beats one you do once a month.
2. **How long does it take each time?** Multiply by frequency to see the hours per week.
3. **What does a mistake cost?** Some tasks are slow but harmless. Others, like forgetting to delist a sold item, cost money and reputation.

Tasks that score high on all three are your first projects. In our experience, these five usually come out on top.

## 1. Photo processing and file naming

Every item needs photos, and every photo needs cropping, brightening, resizing and a sensible file name. Doing this one by one is slow and inconsistent.

Automation can apply the same edits to a whole batch, name files by SKU and put them in the right folder, so they are ready to attach to listings. The result is faster *and* more consistent photos.

## 2. Listing drafts from templates

Much of a listing is predictable: brand, category, size, condition wording, shipping and return details. Instead of typing it again, a template system fills in what it can from your inventory data and leaves you to add what is unique about the item.

This cuts listing time per item and reduces errors such as wrong sizes or missing measurements.

## 3. Delisting and inventory sync across marketplaces

If you sell on more than one marketplace, this is often the task with the highest cost of mistakes. When an item sells on one site, it must be removed from the others quickly, or you risk selling it twice.

Automated sync watches for sales and ends the matching listings, and keeps quantities and prices aligned. We cover this in detail in our [crosslisting guide](/blog/crosslisting-without-double-selling/).

## 4. Order, shipping and customer notifications

Between "sold" and "delivered" there are many small steps: telling the packer what to pick, buying a label, sending tracking, answering "where is my order?".

Simple automations can send each new order to a pick list with the item's shelf location, notify the right person, and post tracking updates. Your team spends less time checking screens and more time shipping.

## 5. Sales, fees and profit reporting

Many resellers know their revenue but not their profit per item, per source or per marketplace, because the numbers are spread across several platforms and payment reports.

An automated report pulls sales, fees, shipping costs and item cost together every day or week. It shows which sources and categories make money, which items sit too long, and where fees eat your margin. Better buying decisions usually follow quickly.

## Start small, then connect the pieces

You do not need one big system on day one. Start with the single task that scores highest for you, measure the hours it saves, and then connect the next one. Over time these small tools become one workflow: from buying, to listing, to selling, to reporting.
