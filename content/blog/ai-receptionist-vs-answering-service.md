Every small business owner hits the same wall sooner or later: the phone rings while you are with a customer, on a ladder, or asleep. You have three main ways to handle those calls. Let them go to **voicemail**, pay a **live answering service**, or use an **AI receptionist**. Each one is the right answer for some businesses and the wrong one for others.

Here is an honest comparison: cost, strengths, weaknesses, and the legal points to check first.

## Quick comparison

| | Voicemail | Live answering service | AI receptionist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical cost | Usually included with your phone plan | Monthly plans by minutes or calls | Monthly plans by calls or minutes, often lower per call |
| Who answers | Nobody, a recording | A trained person | Software with a natural voice |
| Hours | 24/7 | Depends on the plan; many offer 24/7 | 24/7 |
| Best at | Simple callbacks | Judgment, empathy, unusual calls | Repeat questions, intake, booking |
| Main weakness | Some callers hang up | Cost rises with volume | Can misunderstand unusual requests |

## Voicemail

**Cost:** Voicemail usually comes with your phone line. Business phone apps also include it. For example, Google lists [Google Voice for Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/products/voice/) from $10 per user per month (published price as of October 2026; a Workspace plan is billed separately).

**Pros:**

- Free or nearly free, with nothing to set up beyond a greeting.

**Cons:**

- Some callers hang up without leaving a message, and you never learn what they wanted.
- It can't answer even simple questions like your hours or service area.

**Fits when:** you get few calls, most callers are existing customers, and you can return calls within an hour or two.

## Live answering service

A live answering service (or virtual receptionist) uses real people to answer calls in your business name. They take messages, transfer calls, and on some plans book appointments.

**Cost:** Pricing is usually by the minute or by the call, with a monthly bundle and overage fees. Some published examples, as of October 2026:

- Ruby's [plans and pricing page](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) lists plans starting at about $250 per month for 50 receptionist minutes, up to about $1,725 per month for 500 minutes.
- Ruby's own guide says answering service costs in the market [range from about $50 to $450 per month or more](https://www.ruby.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/), and warns that some providers add separate fees for things like setup or call transfers.
- Smith.ai's homepage describes its [human virtual receptionist service](https://smith.ai/) at roughly $6 to $10 per call.

Prices change often, so check the current pricing page and ask what counts as a billable call or minute (for example, spam calls, wrong numbers and hang-ups).

**Pros:**

- A real person can handle upset callers, emergencies and calls that don't fit a script.
- Callers who dislike talking to software get a human.

**Cons:**

- Cost grows with call volume and call length.
- Agents handle many businesses, so their knowledge of yours is limited to the notes you give them.
- Quality can vary from agent to agent.

**Fits when:** calls are high-stakes or emotional (medical, legal, urgent home repairs), call volume is moderate, and a human voice matters to your customers.

## AI receptionist

An AI receptionist answers in a natural voice, understands what the caller says, and responds using information you provide: services, hours, prices, service area, booking rules. It can take a message, book an appointment, or transfer the call to you, and then send you a written summary.

**Cost:** Pricing is often per call or per month. As one published example, Smith.ai's [AI receptionist pricing page](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) lists a free tier with 25 calls per month and paid plans from $150 per month for 75 calls (as of October 2026). Smith.ai's homepage puts its AI receptionist at roughly $1 to $2 per call, compared with $6 to $10 for its human receptionists. Other providers price differently, and some charge setup fees, so compare total monthly cost at your real call volume.

**Pros:**

- Answers every call at once, 24/7, including several calls at the same time.
- Consistent answers and a written summary of every call.
- Usually costs less per call than a live service.

**Cons:**

- It can misunderstand accents, background noise or unusual requests.
- It only knows what you tell it. Outdated info means wrong answers.
- Some callers don't want to talk to software. You need a clear way to reach a human.
- It needs setup and testing before it goes live, and occasional updates after.

**Fits when:** you get many routine calls (quotes, bookings, hours, "do you serve my area?"), you miss calls after hours, and you want every caller to get an answer right away.

## Legal and ethical points to check

None of this is legal advice. Rules depend on where you and your callers are, so talk to a lawyer if you are unsure. Here are the points we think every business should check.

### Tell callers they are talking to an AI

We think an AI receptionist should say it is an AI at the start of the call. It is the honest thing to do, and some states already require disclosure in certain situations:

- **California:** [Business and Professions Code section 17941](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17941.) makes it unlawful to use a bot to communicate online with a person in California with intent to mislead them about its artificial identity in order to drive a sale, unless the bot discloses that it is a bot.
- **Utah:** [Utah Code 13-77-103](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title13/Chapter77/C13-77-S103_2025050720250507.html) requires a business using generative AI with consumers to disclose that the person is talking to AI if they ask, and requires a disclosure at the start in certain high-risk regulated services.

A simple opening line covers this: "Hi, you've reached [Business]. I'm an AI assistant. How can I help?"

### Call recording consent

Many AI receptionists and answering services record calls or create transcripts. Recording laws differ by state:

- **New York** is a one-party consent state. Under [New York Penal Law section 250.00](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.00), recording a conversation is illegal eavesdropping only when done without the consent of at least one party. Eavesdropping is a felony under [section 250.05](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/250.05).
- **California** generally requires the consent of all parties to record a confidential communication, under [California Penal Code section 632](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632).
- Other states have their own rules, and some of them are closer to California's than to New York's.

Because callers can be in any state, the safe practice is to announce recording at the start of every call: "This call may be recorded and transcribed." Then let callers ask for a human or end the call.

### Outbound calls are different

If you want an AI to **make** calls, the rules are much stricter. The FCC ruled in 2024 that [AI-generated voices count as "artificial" voices](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal) under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, which means such calls generally need the person's prior consent. The FCC's guide on [unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) covers the consent rules.

### Protect caller information

Ask any provider where recordings and transcripts are stored, who can access them, and how long they are kept. Never have an AI or an answering service take card numbers over the phone. Send a secure, hosted payment link instead.

## How to choose in four steps

1. **Count your calls.** Check your call log for two weeks: total calls, missed calls, and when they happen.
2. **Sort them.** What share are routine (hours, prices, booking) and what share need a person (complaints, emergencies, complex quotes)?
3. **Price it at your volume.** Take your real monthly calls and average call length, and work out the total cost for each option, including setup and overage fees.
4. **Try it before you commit.** Call the provider's demo line, or run a trial on after-hours calls first. Listen to recordings and read summaries before you send all calls through it.

You can also combine options, for example an AI receptionist for routine and after-hours calls with transfer to a person for anything sensitive. For more on missed calls, see our post on [missed calls and small businesses](/blog/missed-calls-small-business/).
