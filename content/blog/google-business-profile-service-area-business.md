If you are a plumber, cleaner, landscaper, electrician, mobile detailer or any other business that goes to the customer, Google calls you a **service-area business**. You can still show up in Google Search and Maps when people nearby look for what you do. But the setup is a little different from a shop with a front door, and a few wrong choices can get a profile suspended.

This guide walks through each part of the profile, step by step, based on Google's own help pages. Everything here is free to do yourself.

## First, decide what kind of business you are

Google sorts local businesses into three types:

- **Storefront:** customers come to your address, and you have staff there during your hours.
- **Service-area business:** you visit or deliver to customers, and they don't come to you.
- **Hybrid:** you do both, for example a repair shop that also does house calls.

This choice matters. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a service-area business should have one profile for its central office or location, with a service area, and should hide its address from customers. If you work from home and customers never visit, you are a service-area business, and you should not show your home address.

## Step 1: Create the profile and hide your address

Go to [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) and sign in with the Google account you want to own the profile. Use a business account you will keep, not a staff member's personal one.

When Google asks for an address, enter the real address your business runs from. Then make sure customers don't see it. Google's page on [managing your business address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en) explains that you turn off **"Show business address to customers"** under Edit profile, then Location. Google says you should only hide your address if you are a service-area business, and that once it is hidden, your profile shows only your service area.

## Step 2: Set your service areas

Google's help page on [service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) gives the rules:

- You can add **up to 20 service areas**.
- You set them by **city, ZIP code or other named area**, not as a radius around your address.
- Your overall service area **shouldn't extend more than about 2 hours of driving time** from where your business is based.

A practical approach: list the towns or ZIP codes where you really want work and can arrive on time. For a Long Island contractor, that might be a set of towns in one or two counties, not "New York State." A tight, honest list matches what customers expect and stays within Google's rules.

## Step 3: Get verified

Google needs to confirm that your business is real before your profile shows publicly. One of the methods Google may offer is a short video. Google's page on [video verification](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en) says the video must be recorded live on a phone, in one continuous, unedited take of at least 30 seconds. For a service-area business, it suggests showing:

- **Where you are:** street signs or landmarks near your base.
- **That the business operates:** your tools, equipment, branded vehicle or workspace.
- **That you manage it:** for example, unlocking your work van, or a business document that matches your profile name.

Google also says not to show faces or sensitive details like bank, tax or ID numbers, and not to put up temporary signs to make a location look like a storefront. Review can take up to five business days.

## Step 4: Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** is one of the strongest signals of what you do. Google's [category help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en) says to pick the most specific category that describes your business as a whole. You can't create your own.

Then add a few **additional categories** for other main services. Google's test is simple: the category should complete the sentence "This business **is** a ___," not "This business **has** a ___." The [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) also say to use the fewest categories needed to describe your core business. More is not better.

## Step 5: Fill in the name, services and description

- **Business name:** use your real name, the one on your van, invoices and website. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) don't allow adding marketing taglines, phone numbers, locations or other extra words to the name. "Smith Plumbing" is fine. "Smith Plumbing Best Emergency Plumber Nassau County" is not.
- **Services:** Google's [services help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455399) lets you pick suggested services for your category, add your own, and add short descriptions and prices. List the jobs people actually search for.
- **Description:** Google [limits it to 750 characters](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13682007?hl=en) and asks you to focus on your business, not on promotions or prices. Say what you do, who you serve, and where.
- **Hours, phone and website:** keep these accurate. Use a phone number that is answered during the hours you list.

## Step 6: Add real photos

Photos show customers that you are a real business. Google's [photo help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop) lists the basics: JPG or PNG files between 10 KB and 5 MB, with 720 by 720 pixels recommended and 250 by 250 pixels as the minimum. Photos should be in focus, well lit, and without heavy filters or major edits.

Good photos for a service-area business:

- Your logo and a cover photo.
- Your team at work (with their permission) and your branded vehicle.
- Before-and-after shots of real jobs, without customers' house numbers or personal details.

Add new photos from time to time as you finish jobs.

## Step 7: Ask for reviews the right way

Reviews matter both to customers and to Google. Google's page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) says results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that review count and rating are among the signals for prominence. Google also says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

Asking for reviews is allowed. Google's [tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) suggest sharing a review link or QR code with customers. What is not allowed, per Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en):

- Offering discounts, gifts or anything else in exchange for a review.
- Asking only happy customers for reviews (often called "review gating").
- Discouraging or blocking negative reviews.
- Setting quotas for staff to collect a certain number of reviews.

Federal rules apply too. The FTC's [rule on fake reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), in effect since October 2024, bans fake reviews, buying reviews, incentives tied to positive or negative reviews and undisclosed reviews by insiders such as owners or employees, and allows civil penalties for knowing violations.

A simple, compliant habit: after every finished job, send each customer the same short message with your review link. Then reply to reviews. Google's page on [managing customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en) explains how to reply once you are verified. Keep replies short and polite, never share a customer's private details, and for problems, invite the customer to call or email you.

## Step 8: Post updates

Posts let you share news on your profile. Google's [posts help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en) describes three types: **Updates**, **Offers** and **Events**. You can schedule posts ahead of time, and posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range.

Ideas that work for service businesses:

- A seasonal reminder, such as gutter cleaning in the fall or AC tune-ups before summer.
- A recent project with a photo.
- A real limited-time offer with clear dates and terms.

One post a week or every two weeks is a pace most owners can keep.

## A 30-minute checklist

1. Confirm you are set up as a service-area business with your address hidden.
2. Check that your service areas are real places you serve, within about two hours.
3. Review your primary and additional categories.
4. Make sure your name matches your real-world name with no extra words.
5. Add five recent photos.
6. Send your review link to your last ten customers.
7. Reply to any unanswered reviews.
8. Publish one post.
