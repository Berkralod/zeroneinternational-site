Most small business owners don't mind doing the work. What wears them down is what comes after: writing the invoice at night, remembering who hasn't paid, and sending that awkward "just checking in" message for the third time.

The good news is that most of this can run on its own. Not with a big software project, but with a simple routine: send the invoice right away, and let polite reminders go out on a schedule. This post walks through that routine and gives you three reminder messages you can copy today.

## Why a routine beats chasing

Late payments usually aren't about bad customers. More often the invoice arrived late, got buried in an inbox, or was hard to pay. A routine fixes the parts you control:

- **Timing.** An invoice sent the day the job is done reaches the customer while the work is fresh in their mind.
- **Clarity.** An invoice with the right amount, the right job and a due date is easier to approve. Even the U.S. Small Business Administration's [guide for government contractors](https://www.sba.gov/federal-contracting/contracting-guide/governing-rules-responsibilities) puts it simply: the more accurate your invoices, the more quickly you'll be paid.
- **Ease.** A payment link inside the invoice means the customer can pay in a minute, from their phone.
- **Consistency.** Reminders that go out on schedule don't depend on you remembering on a busy week.

## Step 1: Invoice the day the job is done

The best time to send an invoice is the same day you finish the work. Two habits make that possible:

1. **Start from a template.** Your business details, payment terms and the usual line items should already be filled in. Only the job details change.
2. **Tie the invoice to "job done".** Whether you mark jobs finished in a calendar, a job sheet or a simple form, that moment can create the invoice draft for you.

You still check the draft before it goes out. Automation removes the copying and pasting; it doesn't take the decision away from you.

## Step 2: Add a payment link

Make paying the easiest part of the process. Hosted payment pages from providers such as Square, Stripe or PayPal let customers pay by card or bank without you ever handling card numbers. Put the link in the invoice and in every reminder.

## Step 3: Set three polite reminders

Most invoice tools can send reminders before, on and after the due date. [Square Invoices](https://squareup.com/us/en/invoices/features/invoice-reminders) lets you choose that timing and stops reminding once the invoice is paid, and [Stripe Invoicing](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/send-email) can send reminder e-mails for unpaid invoices as well. Three messages are usually enough.

**Three days before the due date:**

> Hi [Name], a quick heads-up that invoice #1042 for [job] is due on Friday. You can pay here: [payment link]. Thank you!

**On the due date:**

> Hi [Name], just a friendly reminder that invoice #1042 is due today. Here's the payment link again: [payment link].

**A few days after the due date:**

> Hi [Name], invoice #1042 may have slipped through. If you have any questions about it, just reply to this message and we'll sort it out. [payment link]

Notice what these messages don't do: they don't threaten, guilt or lecture. They make it easy to pay and easy to ask a question. If there's a problem with the work or the amount, you want to hear about it early, not after the fifth reminder.

## Step 4: Stop when it's paid

Nothing annoys a customer more than a reminder for a bill they already paid. Make sure reminders stop automatically when a payment comes in. If a customer pays outside your invoice tool, by check or cash, mark the invoice paid right away so the reminders end.

## Step 5: Know who still owes you

Once a week, look at one list: unpaid invoices, sorted by how late they are. For anything well past due, a short personal phone call usually works better than another automated message. The point of the routine is that you only make those calls for the few invoices that really need them.

## A simple checklist

- Invoice template with your details and payment terms
- Invoice created the day the job is marked done
- Payment link in every invoice and reminder
- Reminders three days before, on the due date, and a few days after
- Reminders stop automatically when paid
- A weekly look at unpaid invoices
