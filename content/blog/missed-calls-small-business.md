When a customer calls a small business, they usually want something now: a quote, an appointment, a plumber before the basement floods. If nobody picks up, many of them simply call the next name on the list. You never see that lost job in a report. It just doesn't happen.

This post looks at what published call data says about missed calls, and then walks through the practical options for answering more of them, from a better voicemail greeting to an AI receptionist. Most of the first steps cost nothing.

## What the data says about missed calls

Good numbers on missed calls are harder to find than you might think. Many figures that get passed around online have no clear source. Here are the ones we could trace back to the company that published them.

- **About half of calls get a person.** Invoca, a call tracking company, says in its [2026 Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report](https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026) that across more than 70 million calls tracked on its platform, about 56% of calls to businesses were answered by a person. Its [home services report](https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026) puts that sector at 52%.
- **A large share go unanswered.** CallRail, another call tracking company, writes that [28% of inbound calls go unanswered on average](https://www.callrail.com/blog/how-to-close-missed-call-customer-gap) across its customers. CallRail counts a call as missed when it goes to automatic voicemail or the caller hangs up.
- **Few teams think they never miss calls.** In a CallRail survey of more than 600 small business employees, [only 13% said customer and prospect calls are never missed](https://www.callrail.com/blog/callrails-lead-center-now-powers-more-small-to-midsize-businesses).

Two honest caveats. First, both companies sell call software, so they have a stake in the topic. Second, their numbers come from their own customers, not from every business in the country. Still, two separate data sets point the same way: a meaningful share of calls to businesses never reach a person.

The more useful number is your own. You can find it in an afternoon.

## Step 1: Measure your own missed calls this week

Before you buy anything, find out how big the problem is for you.

1. **Check your phone system's call log.** Most business phone services, and most cell phones, show missed calls. Count the missed calls for the last two weeks.
2. **Note when they happen.** Write down the time of day for each missed call. Most businesses find a pattern: lunch, early morning, evenings, weekends, or the hours when everyone is out on jobs.
3. **Check how many callers left a message.** Compare missed calls with voicemails. The gap is the number of people who hung up without telling you what they wanted.
4. **Call yourself.** From another phone, call your business at a busy time and after hours. Listen to what a customer hears.

Now you know whether you have a small leak or a big one, and when it happens. That tells you which fix fits.

## Step 2: Fix the voicemail you already have

Voicemail is free and you already have it. Most greetings can still do a better job.

- **Say your business name first**, so the caller knows they reached the right place.
- **Give a clear callback promise** you can keep, such as "We return calls within two business hours."
- **Ask for the right details**: name, number, address or ZIP code, and what they need. This lets you call back ready to quote.
- **Offer another path**: "You can also book online at our website" or "Text this number for a faster reply."
- **Keep it under 30 seconds.** Long greetings lose people.

Then build the habit that matters most: check voicemail on a schedule and call back fast. A good greeting does little if callbacks take a day.

## Step 3: Use call forwarding so calls reach someone

Many missed calls happen because the one person who answers is busy. Call forwarding can send those calls to someone else.

- **Forward on no answer.** Most carriers and business phone systems let you forward calls that ring a set number of times without being picked up. Ask your carrier how to turn on "forward when unanswered" or "conditional call forwarding" for your line.
- **Ring several phones at once.** Many business phone services can ring the office line and one or two cell phones at the same time.
- **Set hours.** Some systems can route calls one way during business hours and another way after hours.

A business phone app can make this easier. For example, Google lists [Google Voice for Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/products/voice/) at $10, $20 and $30 per user per month for its Starter, Standard and Premier tiers (published prices as of October 2026; a Workspace plan is billed separately). Your current carrier may already include forwarding at no extra cost.

## Step 4: Send a quick text when a call is missed

Some phone systems can send an automatic text when a call goes unanswered: "Sorry we missed your call. This is [Business]. What can we help with?" For some callers, a text is easier than playing phone tag.

Keep these texts to a direct reply about that call. Do not add the caller to marketing texts without permission. The FCC's guide on [unwanted robocalls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) explains that commercial automated texts generally need the person's prior written consent, and people can opt out at any time. If you plan anything beyond a simple reply, check the rules first.

## Step 5: Decide whether you need help answering

If the steps above don't close the gap, it is time to look at outside help. There are two main options.

**A live answering service** uses trained people to answer your calls, take messages, and sometimes book appointments. Pricing is usually by the minute or by the call. As one example, Ruby's [published plans](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) start at about $250 per month for 50 receptionist minutes (as of October 2026). Ruby's own guide says answering service costs in general [range from about $50 to $450 per month or more](https://www.ruby.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/), depending on volume and features.

**An AI receptionist** uses software to answer the call in a natural voice, answer common questions, take details, and send you a summary. Pricing is often per call or per month. For example, Smith.ai's [AI receptionist pricing page](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) lists a free tier with 25 calls a month and a Pro plan at $150 per month for 75 calls (published prices as of October 2026).

We compare these options in more detail, including legal points about disclosure and call recording, in our post on [AI receptionist vs. answering service vs. voicemail](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service/).

## A simple way to choose

Use what you learned in Step 1:

- **A few missed calls a week, mostly when you are busy:** improve voicemail, set up forwarding, and add a missed-call text.
- **Many missed calls after hours or on weekends:** look at an answering service or an AI receptionist that covers those hours.
- **Calls that need judgment, empathy, or emergency triage:** a live person, either on your team or at an answering service, may be the safer choice for those calls.
- **Lots of repeat questions (hours, prices, service area, booking):** an AI receptionist can handle these well and pass the rest to you.

Whatever you choose, review your call log again in a month. If the number of missed calls and hang-ups went down, it is working. If not, adjust.
