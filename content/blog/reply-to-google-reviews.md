Most owners read their new Google reviews. Far fewer reply to them. That is a missed chance, because the people reading your reviews are usually future customers, not the person who wrote the review.

This post covers why replies matter, what Google itself recommends, and example replies you can adapt for good, mixed and bad reviews. Everything here is free to do yourself in a few minutes a week.

## Why replying is worth your time

Two facts explain it.

- **Customers read reviews and look at how you answer.** In BrightLocal's [Local Consumer Review Survey 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 97% of consumers said they read reviews for local businesses, and 80% said they are more likely to use a business that replies to all of its reviews.
- **Google sees replies as part of good profile care.** Google's page on [managing customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en) says helpful, positive replies can show that you value your customers and their feedback.

A reply is public. It is written for the next hundred people who read the review, not only for the reviewer.

## How to reply on Google

You need a verified Business Profile. Then, per [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en):

1. Open your Business Profile on Google Search or Maps (signed in to the account that manages it).
2. Choose **Read reviews**.
3. Next to the review, select **Reply**, write your answer and submit it.

Google checks replies against its content policies before they appear. That usually takes about ten minutes, and in some cases longer. You can edit or delete your reply later.

## Google's own tips for better replies

Google's [review management page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en) lists a few simple rules. In plain words:

- **Keep it short.** People may stop reading a long reply before they reach the main point.
- **Be specific, not generic.** Instead of the same "Thank you!" under every review, reply where you can add something useful: an answer, an update or a detail.
- **Don't sell.** The reviewer is already a customer. Leave deals and promotions out of replies.
- **With negative reviews, stay calm and polite.** Don't share private details about the customer, acknowledge what went wrong without taking blame for things outside your control, and offer to continue by phone or email.

Two more habits from our own work: reply within a few days while the visit is still fresh, and sign with a first name so the reply sounds like a person.

## Example replies you can adapt

These are templates, not real reviews. Replace the parts in brackets with real details, and never copy the same reply under every review.

### A five-star review

> Thank you, [first name]! We're glad the [service] was done before your [event or deadline]. See you at the next one. – [Your name], [Business name]

Why it works: it mentions what the customer actually wrote about, and it is short.

### A five-star review with no text

> Thanks for the five stars, [first name]. We appreciate you taking a minute to rate us. – [Your name]

A one-line thank-you is enough. There is nothing more to answer.

### A good review with a small complaint

> Thank you, [first name], and thanks for the note about [the wait / parking / the price list]. You're right, and we've [what you changed]. Hope to see you again soon. – [Your name]

Why it works: it shows that feedback leads to a real change. Only write this when you really changed something.

### A question inside a review

> Thanks, [first name]! To answer your question: yes, we [answer, for example "now take appointments on Saturdays"]. – [Your name]

Answering here helps every future reader who has the same question.

### A negative review about a real mistake

> [First name], we're sorry. [The order was late / the job wasn't finished on time], and that's on us. We'd like to make it right. Please email us at [address] or call us, and ask for [name]. – [Your name], [role]

Why it works: it is calm, owns the mistake, and moves the details to a private channel.

### A negative review you don't recognize

> Thanks for the feedback. We can't find a visit under this name, and we'd like to understand what happened. Please email us at [address] so we can look into it. – [Your name]

Don't argue in public and don't accuse anyone. If a review breaks Google's rules (for example, it is spam or not about a real experience), you can [report it to Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773?hl=en) instead.

## What to avoid

- **Sharing private details** such as appointment dates, prices paid or health information in a reply.
- **Copy-paste replies** under every review. Readers notice.
- **Offers in exchange for reviews.** Google's [policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) does not allow incentives for reviews, and the FTC's [rule on fake reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) bans buying reviews and incentives tied to positive or negative reviews.
- **Arguing.** Even when the review is unfair, a short, polite reply reads better to the next customer than a long defense.

## A ten-minute weekly routine

1. Open **Read reviews** on your Business Profile.
2. Reply to every new review, starting with the negative ones.
3. Check that older replies still match what you offer today.
4. Note any complaint that came up twice. That is a process to fix, not a review to answer.
5. Send your review link to the customers you served this week, all of them, not only the happy ones.
