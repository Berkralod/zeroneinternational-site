Most people meet your business on a small screen. They search on their phone, tap your site, and decide in a few seconds whether to call, ask for a quote or move on. Google looks at your site the same way: it uses the [mobile version of a site's content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing) for indexing and ranking.

So the question isn't "do we have a mobile site?" It's "does our site actually work on a phone?" Here is a 10-point check you can do yourself in about fifteen minutes. Grab your phone, open your website, and go through the list.

## 1. The first screen says what you do and where

Without scrolling, a visitor should see what you do and where you work. "Plumbing and drain repair in Queens" beats a big photo with a slogan. If someone has to scroll or open a menu to find out, many won't.

## 2. Text is readable without zooming

Body text should be comfortable to read at arm's length, and lines shouldn't run off the edge of the screen. If you catch yourself pinching to zoom, so will your customers.

## 3. Buttons are easy to tap

Fingers are bigger than mouse pointers. The accessibility standard [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) sets a minimum target size of 24 by 24 CSS pixels for things you tap, unless there's enough space around them. In practice: buttons should be easy to hit with a thumb, and links shouldn't sit so close together that you tap the wrong one.

## 4. Calling takes one tap

A phone number written as plain text forces people to copy it. A proper call button opens the dialer in one tap. Check that it works, and that it's visible on the first screen or in a header that stays on screen.

## 5. Directions take one tap

If customers come to you, a "Get directions" button or a map link should open their maps app. If you go to them, say which towns or neighborhoods you serve.

## 6. The quote or booking form is short

Long forms are hard to fill in on a phone. Ask only for what you need to reply: name, a way to reach them, the service, and a short message. Test it yourself: fill it in on your phone and make sure the confirmation message appears and the request reaches you.

## 7. Pages load fast

Speed has a clear yardstick. Google's [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) are three numbers:

- **Loading (LCP):** the main content appears within **2.5 seconds**.
- **Response (INP):** a tap gets a reaction in **200 milliseconds or less**.
- **Stability (CLS):** the layout doesn't jump while people read, with a score of **0.1 or less**.

Each one is judged at the 75th percentile of real page loads. You can check your site for free with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/). Look at the mobile result first. Google's own guide to [Core Web Vitals and Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) recommends that site owners aim for good scores.

Common culprits on small business sites are huge photos, too many plugins, and pop-ups that load before the content.

## 8. The phone version has the same content

Some sites hide sections, prices or reviews on phones to "keep it simple". Since Google uses the mobile version for indexing, its [mobile-first indexing guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing) recommends that the mobile site has the same content as the desktop site. Simplify the layout, not the information.

## 9. Hours and prices are current

Out-of-date hours are one of the fastest ways to lose a customer's trust. Make sure the hours, prices and services on your site match your Google Business Profile, and update both before holidays.

## 10. It's readable for everyone

A few quick accessibility checks help every visitor, including people reading outside in bright sun:

- **Contrast:** WCAG 2.2 asks for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text. Light gray text on white usually fails.
- **Alt text:** photos that carry information (your team, your work, your storefront) should have a short text description.
- **Real text:** don't put important information only inside images.

## Your 10-point checklist

- What you do and where, on the first screen
- Text readable without zooming
- Buttons easy to tap with a thumb
- Call button that works in one tap
- Directions or service area in one tap
- A short quote or booking form that you've tested
- Good Core Web Vitals on mobile (LCP, INP, CLS)
- The same content on phone and desktop
- Current hours and prices, matching your Google profile
- Good contrast, alt text, and real text
